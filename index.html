Carved sculpture and slipcast ornaments, straight from the bench. Some of these are unfired bisque — that is what they look like before the kiln.

The making

Slow, hands-on, and repeated

Every stage is slow and none of them can be skipped. Casting the plaster, pouring the slip, easing the piece out, trimming the seam, cleaning it, glazing it — each one leaves a mark on the result.

The mould designs we use are the work of Old Forge Creations, and we are grateful for them. The plaster, the casting, the trimming and the glaze are Alesha's.

Two ornaments from the same mould on the same afternoon will not come out of the kiln alike. That is the whole point of making them this way.

The studio

Two people, one small studio in Staunton

Alesha

The artist. She builds the forms, chooses what to make, and decides how each piece should look. She also holds a full-time office job, and does this as well — the photographs here were taken across a lot of late evenings.

Jesse

Alesha's partner. Keeps the kiln running, shoots the video, writes it down, and makes ceramic ornaments as well. The ceramics are hers.
