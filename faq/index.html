FAQ

Fair questions, straight answers

What people ask about handmade ceramics, slipcasting, and this studio — answered plainly, with nowhere to hide.

What is slipcasting?

Liquid clay — slip — is poured into a plaster mould. The plaster draws the water out and a wall of clay builds against it, and the rest of the slip goes back to the bucket. The piece comes out of the mould soft, and everything after that — trimming, carving, glazing — happens by hand at the bench.

Is every piece really one of a kind?

The moulds give the starting form, and no two pieces finished here come out identical. Trimming, carving, and glazing are all hand work, and the glaze never lands the same way twice. The fingerprints stay.

Where do the designs come from?

The ornament designs come from Old Forge Creations, who publish 3D files for exactly this kind of piece, and we say so plainly. The plaster moulds are made here in the studio, and every finished ornament is developed here — the wings, the ears, the expressions are all bench work.

Why does handmade ceramics cost more than the mass-made version?

One person, many stages, and days between them. A cast piece is dried slowly, fired, glazed by hand, and fired again before it can be anyone's. What you are paying for is the time — and the fact that a person was here.

How do I care for handmade pieces?

Wash them gently by hand, keep them away from sudden heat and cold, and give the glaze the same patience it was made with. Treat them the way you'd treat anything you plan to keep.

What kinds of pieces does the studio make?

Whimsical slipcast pigs and other figures, Jesse's glazed Christmas ornaments, and ceramic planters that are still in development. The seasonal pieces are where the studio's traditions live — the ornaments come back to the bench every year.

Can I buy anything right now?

Not online yet — nothing is for sale here at the moment. The site is the studio's front window, not a shop, and when sales open they will open here first. Until then you can ask when something is ready; Alesha and Jesse read everything that comes in.

Why keep a piece that isn't perfect?

A fingerprint, a glaze that ran, an ear nobody ordered — those are the evidence. A factory can make a flawless mug a million times over. It cannot make one that remembers a person.
