How a piece is made

Slow, hands-on, and repeated

Four stages in order, and the last one is out of your hands. None of them can be rushed, which is why every ornament takes as long as it does.

Stage one

Cast the plaster mould

The plastic forms the plaster is poured into are 3D files by Old Forge Creations. Alesha casts the plaster herself, one mould at a time.

It took more than two months of evenings to cast enough moulds to begin production. Each one is individual, and each is made by hand rather than bought ready-made.

Stage two

Pour the slip

Liquid clay — slip — is poured into the mould, allowed to set, then eased out and trimmed. Every stage leaves its own mark, which is why the same mould never gives the same result twice.

Stage three

Fire it twice

Bisque firing first, glaze firing second. There is no way to shorten it, and clay that goes into the kiln damp will explode — so the drying is not optional.

Some pieces crack, or a handle comes off, or the kiln does something unexpected. Those get repaired with wax resist and refired. It is not efficient, and it is most of the reason some of these pieces exist at all.

Stage four

Glaze, and let it be itself

Every ornament is glazed by hand, and the glaze behaves differently every time — which is the point. Two pieces from the same mould, glazed the same afternoon, will not come out of the kiln alike.
