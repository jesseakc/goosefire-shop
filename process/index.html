How a piece is made

Four stages, in order, and the last one is out of your hands. This is the part of ceramics people find genuinely interesting, which is that you cannot rush any of it.

1. Carve the mould

Roughly forty plaster moulds on one rack, cast by Alesha over more than two months of evenings. The plastic forms they were cast from are 3D files by Old Forge Creations. Half still hold a cast ornament; half have been eased empty.

2. Form and carve

Where a mould gives the basic shape, Alesha does the rest — carved by hand while the clay is still soft enough to take it. The feather pattern and the face are all cut freehand.

3. Fire it twice

Bisque firing first, glaze firing second. Clay that goes into the kiln damp will explode, so the drying is not optional.

4. Repair

Sometimes a piece cracks, or a handle comes off, or the kiln does something unexpected. Those get repaired with wax resist and refired. It is not efficient and it is most of the reason some of these pieces exist at all.
