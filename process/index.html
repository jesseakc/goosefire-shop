Process

How a piece is made

Slipcasting means the clay starts as a liquid. It is poured into a plaster mould, the plaster drinks the water out of it, and what is left is a solid shape that matches the inside of the mould.

Each stage below is a few hours of somebody's hands. The whole thing is slow, and none of it can be hurried by wanting the piece to be finished.

The ornament designs come from Old Forge Creations, who publish 3D files for this kind of work. The plaster moulds are made in this studio.

The stages

  1. The plaster mould

    The mould is what gives the piece its shape. The ornament designs come from Old Forge Creations, who publish 3D files for this kind of work. The plaster moulds are made in this studio, and making enough of them to start production took more than two months.

  2. Pouring the slip

    Liquid clay, called slip, is poured into the plaster mould. The plaster pulls water out of the slip, and a solid layer forms against the inside of the mould.

  3. Turning the piece out

    Once the clay has stiffened enough, the mould is opened and the piece is turned out. On a cast piece it is still attached to a sprue, the small stem of clay that fed the mould.

  4. Trimming and cleaning

    The sprue is cut away and the seam is cleaned off. This is the slowest, least glamorous part, and where a good deal of the character ends up.

  5. Drying and the first firing

    The piece dries fully before it goes in. The first firing, called bisque, hardens the clay so it can be handled without warping.

  6. Glaze

    Glaze goes onto the bisque piece and goes on wet, so it wraps the form rather than sitting on top of it. Where it pools and runs is where the colour ends up varying.

  7. The second firing

    The glaze firing sets the glaze. This is the firing that turns a pale shape into the finished piece, and it is where the kiln has the last word.

After the mould

A cast piece can leave the mould as it came out, or it can be carved, spotted, or given a new expression before it is ever glazed. That part is where Alesha's own taste shows up most.

FAQ

Questions about the process

How long does a piece take?

Days, and most of them are spent waiting on the clay. Casting is quick; drying has to be slow, and two firings bookend everything in between.

What is slip?

Liquid clay. It is poured into a plaster mould, the plaster pulls the water out, and a solid wall of clay builds against the mould. The rest of the slip goes back to the bucket.

Why plaster moulds?

Plaster draws water out of the slip without sticking to the clay, so one mould can give the same form again and again — and whatever happens after that is up to the bench.
