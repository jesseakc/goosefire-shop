Christmas ornaments

Handmade glazed ceramic Christmas ornaments

Cast in plaster moulds made in this studio, trimmed by hand, glazed in small batches, and fired twice. Made in Staunton, Illinois.

Our ceramic Christmas ornaments begin as liquid slip poured into plaster moulds. The ornament designs come from Old Forge Creations, who publish the 3D files; the moulds themselves took more than two months of studio time to make. Every ornament is trimmed, cleaned, and glazed by hand before its two firings.

Jesse's work

Marked and made by hand

Jesse makes the ornaments. Each one is cast, trimmed, and glazed by hand in a small batch, so no two from the same mould come out quite the same.

The casting is the slow part. Liquid clay goes into plaster, the plaster drinks the water out of the slip, and what is left matches the inside of the mould. After drying it gets cut, cleaned, glazed, and fired twice.

The moulds we use were made in this studio over more than two months of work, from designs by Old Forge Creations, who publish the 3D files.

How the ornaments are made

From the bench

How a batch comes together

The mould drinks the water out of the slip and a solid wall of clay forms against it. The piece is turned out, the seam trimmed, the sprue cut away, and then it waits: dry, first firing, glaze, second firing.

Between the trimming and the glaze is where most of the character arrives. The same shape comes out of the mould every time, and the same shape never leaves the studio twice.

FAQ

Questions about the ornaments

Who makes the ornaments?

Jesse does. They share the bench and the kiln with Alesha's pigs, and they are cast, trimmed, and glazed right here.

Where do the ornament designs come from?

Old Forge Creations publishes the 3D designs, and we credit them plainly. The plaster moulds are made in this studio, and every finished ornament is developed at the bench.

Is each batch different?

Every batch comes out of the kiln with its own glaze personality — teal, red, and cream wherever the glaze decided to run.
